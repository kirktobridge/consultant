---
id: A-8
title: Can a build's footprint be predicted from its backlog entry?
status: refuted
raised: 2026-10-15
resolved: 2026-10-19
---

## Assumption

an agent reading an entry's Scope can name the files its build will
touch, well enough that two entries predicted not to overlap won't conflict.

## Why it matters

P17's "non-conflicting" pick rests on it. If predictions miss,
P15's conflict rule still recovers, so the cost is wasted rebuilds, not broken main.

## How to verify

for each merged entry in history, predict touched files from the
entry text as it stood when claimed, then compare with the merge's `--name-only`
diff. Report recall per entry.

## Resolution

Refuted 2026-10-19, by running the check above against the 11 entry merges in
history (P4 R0–R2, P14, P16, P18, P9, P20, P10, P11, P6 R0). For each one, the
agent predicted touched files from the entry text at its claim commit (the merge
base) and the tree at that commit, *before* looking at the diff, then scored
against `git diff --name-only <merge>^1 <merge>`.

- **Recall per entry** ranged from 25% (P16) to 100% (P4 R1, P14, P9). Most
  entries scored 70–90%.
- **What matters to P17 failed.** Of 19 pairs predicted not to overlap, 9 did
  share a file. The misses cluster on files that most builds touch without the
  entry naming them: the schema checker (7 of 11 merges), `ship/SKILL.md` (6), and
  `tests/helpers/fixture.mjs`, `GUIDE.md` and the process, schema and
  session-audit tests (5 each). So the prediction misses most often on the files
  where conflicts happen.
- **Shared files are not proof of conflicts.** 2 of the 9 pairs could be
  replayed onto a common base, and both merged cleanly. The other 7 lie too far
  apart in history to replay. Whether they would have conflicted line by line is
  undetermined.
- **Even perfect prediction would buy little here.** Only 13 of the 55 actual
  merge pairs touch no common file.

Strength: *verified* for the file-overlap figures (computed from git), but with
one predictor run and a contaminated predictor. The agent had seen the current
tree, which biases recall upward, so the refutation stands.

Broke: P17's "non-conflicting" pick as scoped. A file-level footprint picks
pairs that collide about half the time. A rescope, the owner's call, might treat
the hub files as always overlapping, or pick by line-level conflict.
